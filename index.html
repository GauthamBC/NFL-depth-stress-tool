What the ranking actually means

Rank No. 1 has the thinnest current backup-depth profile in this model. The score measures how steep the starter-to-next-up drop-offs are across a roster. It is not the chance of an injury, a prediction of who will lose a player, a game forecast or a playoff probability.

How to read the model

The public-facing numbers stay simple, but every projected replacement and score has an auditable trail.

How a replacement is chosen

The default replacement is the next available player in the latest depth-chart slot. Recent offensive, defensive or special-teams snap share supports the choice. If that slot has no clear active successor, the model falls back to the same position family and flags the lower confidence.

Latest depth chart → remove unavailable players → next player in slot → check recent usage → same-position fallback if needed

How the impact is scored

The starter and next-up player are valued within their position group. The gap is then adjusted for how much the starter actually plays, the leverage of the position and how depleted that unit already is. The final impact is converted to a league-relative 0-100 index.

Depth Cliff × Recent Role Share × Position Leverage × Unit Stress → league-relative impact percentile

NFL basics

Quick glossary

Snap: one play from scrimmage. A player's snap count is how many plays they were on the field for.

Snap share: player snaps divided by the team's total relevant snaps, such as offensive snaps for a receiver.

Depth chart: the team's player order by position, usually first choice, second choice, third choice and so on.

First-choice player: the current player carrying the role after accounting for availability and recent usage.

Next up: the player projected to inherit that role if the first-choice player becomes unavailable.

Special teams (K/P/LS): kicker, punter and long snapper.
